Research

Generative AI has entered classrooms and workplaces faster than the preparation of the people who teach and train in them. My research asks how educators, trainers, and learners can keep human judgment in the loop, and I pursue that question along three connected lines.

Generative AI and critical thinking

With colleagues, I conducted a bibliometric analysis and systematic review of research on GenAI and critical thinking in higher education, published in the Journal of Computers in Education (2026). In a companion study in Education Sciences (2025), I used the Practical Inquiry model of the Community of Inquiry framework to contrast passive, AI-directed use with human–AI supported collaboration. That article has been cited 171 times on Google Scholar, and its findings are available as an interactive research visualization.

These studies led me to design a structured critical AI inquiry model for college students. When I implemented it in online courses, academic integrity concerns in student writing fell from high to low levels, and in bilingual and multicultural education it led to more responsible use and greater ethical awareness.

Through IGNITE-STEM, an NSF FINDERS Foundry project with Tuba City Unified School District on the Navajo Nation, our team co-designed an Ask, Explore, Check, Decide model for AI-supported STEM learning with teachers, families, and community members. We are now validating a critical thinking survey for K–12 STEM students who learn with AI.

Measuring multidimensional AI literacy

AI professional development is often designed without evidence of what educators already know. I developed the Functional, Ethical, Rhetorical, and Pedagogical AI Literacy Scale for Educators (FERPAI), grounded in digital multiliteracies theory and UNESCO’s AI Competency Framework for Teachers. In an initial validation with 239 preservice, K–12, and higher-education educators, the four subscales were reliable (α = .87–.93) and the refined model showed acceptable fit (CFI = .920, SRMR = .059).

Next, I will validate FERPAI separately with preservice teachers, in-service teachers, and university faculty, test measurement invariance across these groups, and adapt the scale for corporate and government trainers.

Designing AI-enhanced professional learning

My dissertation, GenAI Literacy for Language Teachers for a More Responsible and Effective Use of the Technology: Testing, Experiences, and Reflections, is a sequential mixed-methods study of the Teachers in the Loop training. I designed it the way an instructional designer designs workplace training: a baseline needs assessment, then ten microlearning videos, narrated modules, and an avatar-based facilitator for asynchronous, low-bandwidth delivery. Teachers improved significantly on all four literacy dimensions (Journal of Applied Instructional Design, 2026). A companion study examines teachers’ engagement with synthetic presenters.

Related work

I have used narrative ethnography to examine an English learner’s language and inclusion (AERA, 2025) and narrative inquiry to study Grow Your Own teacher-program leaders in rural Arizona (AERA, 2024). As part of a research group at Northern Arizona University, I have also applied social network analysis to online learning communities, examining social presence and interaction patterns.
