About me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Curriculum and Instruction at Northern Arizona University, finishing in May 2027. Before coming to the United States, I prepared future English teachers for more than seven years at Zagazig University in Egypt. I was the first woman from my village to enter higher education and, later, to join the faculty of a College of Education.

My work sits where learning design, AI literacy, and teacher education meet. I design professional learning for teachers, build instruments that measure educators’ AI literacy, and teach online courses in which students feel seen and supported. I am trilingual, in Arabic, English, and French, and I treat the languages and cultures my students bring as assets for everyone’s learning.

Research

Three connected lines of work. Read more on the research page.

Generative AI and critical thinking

How does GenAI shape the way learners question, check, and decide? My systematic review in the Journal of Computers in Education and my Education Sciences study, cited 171 times, examine this question in higher education; you can explore the study’s findings interactively. Through IGNITE-STEM, an NSF-funded project on the Navajo Nation, I am bringing the question to K–12 STEM.

Measuring AI literacy

I developed FERPAI, a scale that measures educators’ functional, ethical, rhetorical, and pedagogical AI literacy. An initial validation with 239 educators is complete; next come separate studies with preservice teachers, in-service teachers, and university faculty.

Designing AI-enhanced professional learning

My dissertation designed and tested the Teachers in the Loop training: microlearning videos, narrated modules, and an avatar-based facilitator. More than 250 teachers, instructional designers, faculty members, and students have completed it.
